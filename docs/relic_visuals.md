# Relic visuals: worn models, grade effects, armour art

How a worn relic looks on a player (1.29.0). Code: `client/relics/RelicWornLayer`, `client/relics/worn/lib/*` (models, catalog, drawing), `client/relics/fx/*` (grade plan, budget, options, particles), `client/relics/ArmorPainter`. Models and textures are generated by `tools/relics/gen_worn_*.py` (run `python3 tools/relics/gen_worn_all.py`), previewed without the game by `tools/relics/preview_worn.py` and `tools/relics/worn_contact_sheet.py` (sheets of every relic in `docs/relic_visuals/`). The three boss renderers (`worn/*Renderer`) are unchanged and win over the library.

## Pipeline
1. A model is built in Python with `tools/relics/mesh_lib.py` (named parts, lofts, lathes, sweeps, smoothed normals) in `gen_worn_head/back/neck/body/sig.py`, authored y-up relative to the pivot of a body part (head, body, right arm, right leg; the left arm is the right one mirrored by the catalog).
2. `gen_worn_lib.write_model` writes `assets/nusmp/relic_worn/<name>.txt`: quads with a colour **slot** (main, trim, glow, dark, accent, none), a material **tile** of one shared atlas (`textures/relic_worn/atlas.png`, grey tileable tiles: metal, gilt, cloth, leather, scale, horn, feather, gem, runes, flame ...), flags (emissive, shell, hot) and an animation group.
3. `catalog.txt` says which models a relic wears: a `relic <id>` entry (signature) or the `look <SLOT> <LOOK>` entry of its slot and look. A relic's palette (main, trim, glow) tints the slots, so one model serves every relic of its look.
4. At run time `WornModels` parses a file once and caches it until the next resource reload; `WornLibrary` resolves a relic to its models once; `WornLibDraw` draws them in three passes (solid, emissive `RenderType.eyes`, shell `entityTranslucentEmissive` with a scrolling shimmer texture) through scratch matrices (no per-frame allocation except one pose push per piece). Pieces follow `ModelPart#translateAndRotate`, so walk swing, sneak and attack swings carry over. Animation groups: bob, spin, orbit, swing (trails with the stride), sway, pulse, wave.
5. A relic the library does not know keeps the old plain boxes. Nothing is drawn in first person (the layer is not run for the local player's own view) nor beyond 32 blocks.

## Grade effects (`WornFxPlan`)
| Grade | Effect |
|---|---|
| NORMAL, SPECIAL | model only |
| RARE | faint rim glow (shell pass, alpha grows towards the silhouette) |
| UNIQUE | stronger rim and a soft shimmer sliding over the shell parts |
| LEGEND | orbiting glow motes (3), the turning rune ring, a gentle pulse of the emissive parts |
| GOD | 5 motes, rune ring, flowing halo band, stronger pulse, a flowing halo of particles over the head |
| GENESIS | 7 motes, all of the above, strongest pulse, End Rod halo |

Only the two best relics of a wearer get motes, rings and halo. Ambient particles (`RelicFxClient`) drift off every worn relic of RARE and up, themed by the catalog (`fx <id> <theme>`) or by the hue of the glow colour (`FxTheme.fromGlow`): ember, snow, spark, leaf, wisp, star, bee, petal.

## Options (client, `config/nusmp-relicfx-client.toml`)
`relicfx.quality` = low / medium (default) / high: the vertex budget per player in view (2400 / 4200 / 6000), which effects exist and how dense the particles are. `relicfx.particles` = false stops the ambient particles. `relicfx.models` = false returns Curios relics to the plain boxes (head and feet armour keep their models because their painted box is left clear).

Budget rules (`WornBudget`): the models of the best grades are drawn while they fit, then their extras. Wearers 16 to 32 blocks away get the models alone; particles only for wearers within 32 blocks and in front of the camera, at most ten wearers and `particlesPerTick` particles per client tick.

## Armour relics (head, chest, feet)
`ArmorPainter` paints the worn layer; 1.29.0 adds a last coat (wear scuffs and glints, lit top edges, dark lower edges, engraved glow marks) and a **glow channel** (`paintGlow`): the pixels in the glow colour are drawn a second time over the armour with `RenderType.eyes` by `ArmorGlow` (vanilla's outer armour model, same size, posed like the wearer). A head or feet relic that has a 3D model gets a clear armour texture (the model stands where the box was); chest relics keep the painted body and gain 3D collars, pauldrons, tails or straps.

## How to add a signature model
1. Write a function decorated with `@model` in a `gen_worn_*.py` file (see `crown_duck` for a figure on a crown, `cloak_levitation` for animated parts); keep it near 1000 vertices.
2. Add one line to `catalog()` in `gen_worn_all.py`: `cat.relic("my_relic_id", "my_model")`, optionally `"deco_bees"` and so on as extra pieces, and `cat.theme("my_relic_id", "ember")`.
3. Run `python3 tools/relics/gen_worn_all.py`, then `python3 tools/relics/worn_contact_sheet.py --ids my_relic_id`, and look at it. `WornMeshTest` parses every file and checks the catalog.

## Why (architecture and balance)
- **Data-driven catalog**: relics are added by waves in Java, the art by one text line, so the look of a new relic needs no Java.
- **Slots and tiles instead of painted models**: one atlas and a palette keep 90 models small (about 5 MB of text, one 128x256 PNG) and let every relic of a look have its own colours.
- **Baked text meshes with smoothed normals**: parsed once, no per-frame maths except animation; the owner's modelling method gives the lofted, shaded look instead of boxes.
- **Three passes**: solid is lit, emissive is full bright, the shell pass is the only translucent one, so the cost of the effects is separable and can be dropped by the budget.
- **Budget and quality option**: the owner's worst case is about 6000 vertices per player; the cap is per quality so slow machines choose cheap, and distance rules remove the extras first.
- **Models replace the armour box for head and feet**: armour sits one pixel outside the body, so a model inside it would be hidden; leaving the texture clear is the least intrusive fix.
- **Glow channel through the armour model**: reusing vanilla's armour model guarantees the glow lands exactly on the painted pixels with no UV code of ours.
- **Particles are client-only and budgeted**: nothing is synchronised; staggering by entity id and a per-tick cap keep the cost flat however many wearers there are.

Not verified in game (no client here): the draw order and winding in the real renderer, the look of the shimmer and rim, the positions against real armour.

## 1.34.0: tooltips never vanish, every worn relic is drawn

**Tooltip.** The relic tooltip is built in three places (`RelicTooltip.onGather`, the component's layout, its drawing); each is now guarded, and a failure falls back to the plain grade and slot lines (`RelicTooltipFallback.safe`, pure and tested) instead of losing the tooltip. `onColor` is guarded too. An unattuned relic used to show only a faint "power sleeps" text (no abilities, no passives); it now shows everything plus that line, so nothing is hidden from the player who holds the item. Discovery (`core/discovery`) has no tooltip hook and hides nothing; there is no "worn" suppression in the tooltip code. The cause of the owner's exact screenshot could not be reproduced without the game: the guarded paths and the faint-tooltip rule are the code-level suspects.

**Worn visibility.**
- `RelicWornLayer` draws every equipped relic with a model. Before, the vertex budget could skip whole models and all relics of one slot/look stood at the same place. Now models are never dropped (only the extras: rim, motes, rings, halo are budgeted), up to 24 relics and 72 pieces.
- `WornPlacer` (pure, tested) is the anchor allocator: slot + index among the relics of that slot (by id, stable while grades change) -> offset, turn, scale, flip. Index 0 is the untouched look. Charms: hip left, hip right, back, back, then lower; necklaces: each lower, forward and smaller; head: stacked above, then to the sides; hands: along the arm; back: layered outward; belts: lower. Plain-box relics take the places after the ones with models.
- Visibility toggle: `CuriosLink.flags / renderFlags / isRendered` read the eye icon of the Curios screen (`ICurioStacksHandler#getRenders`, per slot index) reflectively; true on any doubt. A hidden slot draws no model, no plain box, no custom renderer and no particles. Armour relics (head, chest, feet) have no Curios toggle: client option `relicfx.hideArmourRelics` (plain `define`, default false) and the key "Toggle Relic Visuals" (unbound by default) flip it. The painted armour texture of the item itself stays (vanilla armour rendering).
- Size: all on-body models are drawn at 0.85 (`WornPlacer.BODY_SCALE`); pieces whose model name contains pauldron, shoulder, bracer or gauntlet are pulled 0.7 px toward the body and a little smaller. Head items are untouched.

Not verified in game: the placements (the offsets are estimates against the model files), the Curios reflection against the installed Curios version, the key.
